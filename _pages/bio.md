I'm Scott Wen-tau Yih, a Research Scientist at [FAIR, Meta SuperIntelligence Labs](https://ai.meta.com/research/) in Seattle.
I am also an Affiliate Professor at the [University of Washington](https://www.washington.edu/).
My current research focuses on **multi-agent systems and memory for automated research**, with a particular interest in **recursive self-improvement**: systems that learn from their experiments and improve how they pursue the next question.

This work builds on my research in information retrieval, question answering, and factuality in language models. I was named an [ACL Fellow in 2024](https://www.aclweb.org/portal/content/acl-fellows-2024).

## Current research

### Agents that build on each other's work

How can a group of agents turn individual attempts into cumulative progress? I study systems that coordinate exploration, experimentation, and verification through shared, persistent records of their work.

### Memory for learning and discovery

Memory is central to retaining useful knowledge, revisiting earlier findings, and choosing what to try next. My work spans memory within language models and memory shared across agents. I'm interested in how these capabilities can support research over many rounds of experimentation.

### Automated research and recursive self-improvement

My longer-term aim is to develop research systems that can propose ideas, evaluate them against evidence, and use the results to improve their methods. Grounding those systems in scientific literature connects this direction to my earlier work on retrieval and factuality.

## Background & service

Before joining Meta, I was a Principal Research Scientist at the [Allen Institute for Artificial Intelligence](https://allenai.org) (2017–2019). I worked at [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/) from 2005 to 2017, most recently as a Senior Researcher. I received my PhD from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign in 2005.

My papers have received the KDD 2026 Best Paper Award in the Datasets and Benchmarks track, the CoNLL 2011 Best Paper Award, and an ACL 2015 Outstanding Paper Award. I have served as program co-chair for CEAS 2009, CoNLL 2014, and EMNLP 2021, and as a senior area chair for ACL, NAACL, EMNLP, EACL, ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS.
